A fundamental question of space travel is, "where do we want to go? and what shall we do when we get there?"

  There are not only many places, but many kinds of places we might like to visit in this solar system of ours, and the nature of each suggests what we might do there.

- <img src="/dms-source/files/mw/thumb_2_20_Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_03.png_120px-Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_03.png" width="120" height="68" />

  Mercury gives every sign of being a mineralogical treasurehouse. Alas, it is too close to the Sun to be easily reached.

- <img src="/dms-source/files/mw/thumb_8_89_Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_04.png_120px-Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_04.png" width="120" height="68" />

  Venus, sometimes referred to as Earth's twin, is a bizarre hell-world of largely unknown potential.

- <img src="/dms-source/files/mw/thumb_1_17_Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_05.png_120px-Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_05.png" width="120" height="68" />

  There is one body which has been visited by humans so far. To quote Krafft Ehricke : "If God had meant Man to travel in space, He would have given the Earth an enormous moon."

- <img src="/dms-source/files/mw/thumb_c_c4_Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_06.png_120px-Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_06.png" width="120" height="68" />

  Mars exerts a remarkable power of fascination on the mind.

- <img src="/dms-source/files/mw/thumb_3_3f_Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_07.png_120px-Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_07.png" width="120" height="68" />

  "Inter Iovem et Martem planetam interposui" — Ceres and Pluto are the lead examples of two great classes of minor planets.

- <img src="/dms-source/files/mw/thumb_e_e1_Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_08.png_120px-Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_08.png" width="120" height="68" />

  Jupiter outweighs everything else in the system put together, with the exception (naturally) of the Sun itself. Its four great satellites dwarf any others.

- <img src="/dms-source/files/mw/thumb_a_a2_Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_09.png_120px-Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_09.png" width="120" height="68" />

  Saturn is best known for its ring system, but its moon Titan could be a planet in its own right.

- <img src="/dms-source/files/mw/thumb_a_af_Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_10.png_120px-Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_10.png" width="120" height="68" />

  Uranus is inexplicably heeled over, so that its axis of rotation lies almost in its orbital plane.

- <img src="/dms-source/files/mw/thumb_8_81_Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_11.png_120px-Astronautics_2_our_solar_system_11.png" width="120" height="68" />

  Neptune, like Saturn, has one giant moon, and its name sounds very similar.
